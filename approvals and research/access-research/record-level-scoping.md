# Record-level bill scoping in AP software

Research date: 2026-08-16. Scope: NOT the role/permission-bundle landscape (already researched separately — see `roles-research/`). This file is specifically about **which individual bills/invoices** a person can see and edit, given their role — the axis our current system has zero support for (`bills.view` = every bill, `bills.edit` = every bill).

Products covered: Bill.com, Ramp, Tipalti, Stampli, Melio, QuickBooks Online (QBO), Xero, NetSuite, Coupa, SAP Concur (Invoice).

**How to read confidence markers in this doc:**
- **[SOURCED]** — a specific help-center/support/developer-doc page states this. URL given.
- **[SOURCED — blog/marketing]** — first-party but not help-center documentation (vendor blog, marketing page, developer/API docs describing schema rather than UI). Lower confidence than help-center docs; noted explicitly.
- **[INFERENCE]** — our own reasoning from adjacent sourced facts, not a direct statement in any doc. Flagged inline every time.
- **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]** — we looked and found nothing. This is itself a finding — see the "weakest findings" section — not a stand-in for "assume it doesn't exist."

**Access notes:** `help.bill.com` was unreachable all session (persistent TLS certificate failure on the domain, both via agent fetches and our own direct retries) and Bill.com's marketing URLs guessed as fallbacks 404'd. Bill.com findings below are limited to its public developer/API docs (`developer.bill.com`), which describe schema/API behavior, not confirmed UI behavior — this gap is real and called out per-question. `compass.coupa.com` (Coupa's main help portal) requires customer SSO login and was not accessible; Coupa findings come from its public developer/API docs plus Google-cached snippets. `central.xero.com` blocked plain fetches; Xero findings come from a live browser session that loaded the actual pages. All other products (Ramp, Tipalti — via Zendesk's public API rather than the human-facing help pages, which 403'd —, Melio, QBO, NetSuite, SAP Concur) had directly fetchable help-center or documentation pages.

NetSuite and Bill.com's actual default could not be confirmed either way from available docs (NetSuite's mechanisms exist but their interaction is undocumented; Bill.com's docs were unreachable).

### AP clerk / processor role

- **Ramp**: broader by default — "View all submitted bills and recurring bills" org-wide, full edit/create/delete on drafts; narrows to assigned entities only if multi-entity restriction is turned on. [SOURCED](https://support.ramp.com/accounts-payable-role-on-ramp)
- **Tipalti**: "Process Bills" permission is broad (add/review/approve/PO-match/pay/reconcile) — appears org-wide by default, not bill-assignment-scoped. Separate "AP processor" pre-bundled role exists. [SOURCED](https://help.tipalti.com/hc/en-us/articles/30607338699799-User-permissions)
- **Stampli**: same AP Assignment model as approvers — "Trays" (queues) determine which invoices each processor sees; assignable by business unit/department/entity/region/vendor/office. **[SOURCED — blog/marketing]**
- **Melio**: Accountant/Contributor can edit bills "only if they created" them — creator-scoped, not org-wide-edit by default, though **view** appears unrestricted per the permissions matrix. [SOURCED](https://help.melio.com/hc/en-us/articles/5856324646684-Melio-Roles-Permissions)
- **QBO custom roles**: the "Create" action-permission is explicitly creator-scoped — "View and create new transactions. And modify those transactions, but not those created by others." [SOURCED](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/access-permissions/add-manage-custom-roles-quickbooks-online-advanced/L8Ugph7xl_US_en_US). QBO Bill Pay's "Bill clerk" role can "add bills, mark bills as paid, and add and edit vendors" — feature description only, record-level scope undocumented. [SOURCED](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/manage-workflows/set-use-bill-approval-payment-release-workflows/L1IOLL9hv_US_en_US)
- **Xero**: "Sales and purchases" role sees bills entered by **all users** — no clerk-level scoping exists in native Xero except the separate, non-approving "Draft" role (creator-only). [SOURCED](https://central.xero.com/0/article/Invoice-Only-user-role)
- **NetSuite**: governed by the general Employee/Department/Class/Location/Subsidiary Restrictions on the role — a clerk role can be scoped to "own and subordinates only" or "own, subordinate, and unassigned," same mechanism as any other role. [SOURCED](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/)
- **Coupa / Concur**: processor visibility is Group-scoped by design (same Group primitive as approvers); Concur's "Invoice AP User" defaults to the "Global group" (i.e., everything) only if no supplier groups have been configured — the closest thing to an "org-wide by default, narrows once configured" pattern found in this research. [SOURCED](https://help.sap.com/docs/concur-invoice/concur-invoice-professional-edition-administration-help/invoice-tab)
- **Bill.com**: Clerk role "has permissions to enter bills" — no scope detail beyond that. **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]**

### Accountant / bookkeeper role

Generally documented as **broad/unrestricted** wherever it appears at all: Ramp does not define a distinct Accountant role in the Bill Pay article; Melio's Accountant is edit-scoped to bills it created but view-unrestricted; Xero's Standard/Administrator (accountant-equivalent) has full org-wide visibility; NetSuite's Accountant-type roles are governed by the same general Restrictions mechanism as any role (no special treatment found). No product was found to give the Accountant role a *narrower* default scope than AP Clerk — if anything, several treat it as closer to Admin.

### Plain Viewer role

- **Ramp**: no dedicated Viewer role documented in the Bill Pay-specific article; employees/managers outside the approval chain or vendor-ownership "will not be able to access the Bill Pay tab as a whole" — i.e., Ramp's default for uninvolved users is **zero access**, not read-only-everything. [SOURCED](https://support.ramp.com/managing-bills-and-payments-on-bill-pay)
- **Melio**: Viewer "can view the information in the Melio account but cannot modify it" — documented as unrestricted (all-bills) read-only. [SOURCED](https://help.melio.com/hc/en-us/articles/4418593083932-The-different-user-roles-in-Melio)
- **Xero**: Viewer role confirmed via the role-comparison table to have "Purchases ✔" — org-wide read-only. [SOURCED](https://central.xero.com/0/article/User-roles-and-permissions-in-Xero-Business-edition-GL)
- **QBO, NetSuite, Tipalti, Stampli, Coupa, Concur, Bill.com**: no dedicated "plain Viewer" scoping behavior beyond the general role-restriction mechanisms already described. **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]** as a distinct case.

**Pattern worth noting:** where a distinct "Viewer" exists, it tends to be **org-wide read-only** (Melio, Xero) rather than record-scoped — scoping is something products reserve for roles that *act* on bills (approve/edit), not roles that merely look.

## 2. Scoping dimensions

| Dimension | Common? | Products confirming it (native/documented) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assigned-to-me / routed-to-me** | Yes — the single most universal dimension | Ramp, Tipalti, Stampli, Melio, QBO, Coupa, Concur | The default and near-universal scoping axis for the Approver role specifically (see §1). Xero and possibly Bill.com/NetSuite are exceptions. |
| **Vendor / supplier** | Yes, common | Ramp (Vendor Owner role — "view all bills from any vendor they own"), Melio (workflow trigger condition), Stampli (AP Assignment criterion), Concur (Vendor Employee Access feature explicitly restricts a processor to specific vendors), Coupa (Approval Chain "Applies to Supplier" field) | Used both as a routing *trigger* (who must approve) and, in Ramp/Concur, as a genuine visibility *restriction*. |
| **Dollar amount band** | Yes, common — but usually a routing/threshold trigger, not a visibility filter | Ramp (all plans — a scoping filter, not just a trigger), Tipalti ("Approval Tiers" by amount range + personal approver thresholds), Melio (workflow condition), Concur (Authorized/Cost-Object Approvers keyed to limit), Coupa (native `Minimum/Maximum Total Amount` fields on the Approval Chain object), Bill.com (BILL_AMOUNT / BILL_LINE_ITEM_AMOUNT as approval-policy rule keys) | **Important distinction**: in most products this determines who must approve (a trigger), not who can *see* other people's bills. Ramp is the clearest case where amount is also a plan-gated visibility-scoping dimension in its own right. |
| **Department / cost centre** | Common at the enterprise end, rare/thin at the SMB end | Ramp (Plus tier only), NetSuite (native Restrictions subtab — none/own/own+subordinates/own+subordinates+unassigned), Coupa ("Accounts equals/contains" condition — inferred to map to cost-center/GL, not spelled out by name), Stampli (Trays/Departments as an organizing layer — blog-sourced) | QBO explicitly documents "location" restrictions for the *Sales* area but does not repeat the example for *Bills* — genuinely ambiguous whether it extends there. Melio has no department dimension at all (only amount/creator/vendor). |
| **Legal entity / subsidiary** | Enterprise-only, and one of the better-documented dimensions where it exists | Tipalti (best-documented of all 10 — dedicated multi-entity user access model, "default entity" per user, entity switcher), NetSuite (OneWorld-only; "Allow Cross-Subsidiary Record Viewing" for view-only access outside assigned subsidiaries), Ramp (Plus tier, "Business Entity" scoping + AP role multi-entity restriction toggle) | Xero has **no** multi-subsidiary concept at all — each legal entity is a fully separate Xero org with a separate user list **[INFERENCE from general Xero architecture, not a page we verified this session]**. Melio, QBO, Stampli-help-center, Bill.com, Coupa, Concur: not documented as a native record-visibility dimension (Coupa is known generally for multi-entity support but we could not confirm it maps into Approval Chain conditions). |
| **Project** | Effectively nobody does this for bills specifically | None of the 10 products documented project-level bill-visibility scoping. Xero Projects has an analogous "Edit own only" restriction, but for time entries/tasks, not Bills. | Treat as a non-existent dimension for AP scoping across this set. |
| **Location / office** | Present but usually folded into "department" or used as a workflow trigger, not a distinct axis | Ramp (Plus tier, listed alongside department/entity/vendor/GL), QBO (workflow *trigger* for approval routing — "amount, vendor, location" — not a visibility filter), Stampli (Trays can be named/organized by "region"/office — blog-sourced), NetSuite (Location is one of the three Department/Class/Location restriction fields) | Rarely a first-class, independently named dimension distinct from department. |

**Paid-tier gating, summarized:** Ramp gates department/entity/location/vendor/GL-value scoping to its **Plus** plan (amount-only scoping is on all plans). QBO gates *any* custom-role record scoping (assignment-based approve, creator-based create) to **QBO Online Advanced / Intuit Enterprise Suite** — base QBO plans have no record-level scoping at all. NetSuite's subsidiary restriction is gated to the **OneWorld** edition. Melio's amount/creator/vendor workflow conditions require **Core plan and above** (excluded on the entry-level Go plan). Tipalti, Stampli, Coupa, Concur did not have documented plan-tier gating for their scoping dimensions — for the enterprise tools (Coupa, Concur) this is likely because everything is configuration, not a plan SKU; **[INFERENCE]**, not confirmed by a pricing page.

## 3. Edit rights over the bill's lifetime (the crux)

This is the least-documented question across the entire research set — several products have a real, confirmed gap here, which is itself the most useful finding for your design.

- **Ramp**: yes, if granted the optional "Edit and archive existing (submitted) bills and payments" custom permission — not default, admin-configurable. [SOURCED](https://support.ramp.com/accounts-payable-role-on-ramp)
- **Tipalti**: yes, implied — "An AP processor can add or adjust approvers directly on a bill" (confirms mid-flow modification of the approval chain itself, though this is about approver assignment, not financial fields). Financial-field mid-approval editing for the AP-processor role specifically is not separately spelled out. [SOURCED](https://help.tipalti.com/hc/en-us/articles/38006810185879-Bill-approvers)
- **Melio**: yes, but creator-scoped only ("Edit bill" = ✓ for Accountant/Contributor "only if they created" the bill). [SOURCED](https://help.melio.com/hc/en-us/articles/5856324646684-Melio-Roles-Permissions)
- **Xero**: yes, unrestricted — any field, any time pre-payment, per the "Sales and purchases"/"Purchases" role. [SOURCED](https://central.xero.com/0/article/Edit-bills)
- **NetSuite**: yes — any role with Edit permission on Vendor Bill can edit regardless of approval status; no "locked while pending" state is documented. [SOURCED](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/)
- **Concur**: yes, explicitly and extensively — "Make any necessary modifications to the invoice," including amount, due dates, names, itemization/allocation; full field-edit is specifically confirmed for the **Invoice Processor Manager** sub-role. Vendor can also be changed, with a confirmation prompt. [SOURCED](https://help.sap.com/docs/concur-invoice/concur-invoice-professional-edition-end-user-help/about-processing-invoices), [SOURCED](https://help.sap.com/docs/concur-invoice/concur-invoice-professional-edition-end-user-help/viewing-or-editing-invoice-details)

### Can an admin override and edit anything at any time?

Directly confirmed for **Concur** (Invoice Configuration Administrator role "can fully manage (add, edit, delete)... Invoices and authorised approver workflows" org-wide — [SOURCED](https://help.sap.com/docs/concur-invoice/concur-invoice-professional-edition-administration-help/invoice-tab)) and **Coupa** (explicit `bypass_approvals` / `bypass_current_approval` API actions, described as letting a system "get an invoice approved and paid as quickly as possible" — [SOURCED](https://docs.coupa.com/en/developer-documentation/the-coupa-core-api/resources/transactional-resources/invoices-api-invoices)). For the rest (Ramp, Tipalti, Stampli, Melio, QBO, Xero, NetSuite, Bill.com) an admin's unrestricted override is **[INFERENCE]** from their general "full access" role description, not a page that states an explicit override action the way Coupa and Concur do.

### THE critical sub-question: what happens to already-collected approvals when a material field is edited?

This is the single most decision-relevant finding of the whole research pass, and the results split cleanly into three buckets:

**Bucket 1 — Explicit, hard, non-optional reset (only one product confirms this cleanly):**
- **Ramp**: editing Vendor, payment amount, payment details (method/source/destination account), or payment schedule/date **"will always restart the approval chain and cannot be disabled."** Other, lower-risk field types (e.g. GL coding, description) can be optionally configured by the customer to restart the chain or not. There is no silent-edit path for the three high-risk fields — it's a hard, non-configurable reset, not a warning banner. Additionally, **once a bill is paid**, vendor/entity/total-amount/payment-method become fully locked; changing them requires refund/credit + a brand-new bill rather than an edit. [SOURCED](https://support.ramp.com/bill-pay-approvals), [SOURCED](https://support.ramp.com/bill-lifecycle)

**Bucket 2 — The mechanism exists and is named, but the automatic trigger condition is unconfirmed:**
- **Coupa**: has a literal, named API action — `PUT /api/invoices/:id/restart_approvals` — described as allowing "a 3rd-party system on the customer's side to clear the current approval chain and regenerate a new approval chain for an invoice that has had modifications." This is a strong, direct confirmation that the *concept* you're asking about ("re-routing on change") is a real, designed Coupa behavior, named almost exactly the way you framed it. However: it's documented as an API action ("allows a 3rd-party system... to clear"), and we could **not confirm** whether the standard Coupa UI fires this **automatically** on every material-field edit, or whether it's more commonly admin/integration-triggered after a correction is made. That distinction (auto vs. manual) is **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]**. [SOURCED](https://docs.coupa.com/en/developer-documentation/the-coupa-core-api/resources/transactional-resources/invoices-api-invoices)

**Bucket 3 — Documented evidence pointing toward "nothing happens" (silent edit), inferred from adjacent facts, not stated outright:**
- **Xero**: two directly-sourced facts combine to suggest a silent-edit model — (a) "If there are no part-payments or credit notes applied to the bill, you can edit any field" on an already-approved (awaiting-payment) bill, and (b) "After a bill is approved, the status can't later be changed to Pending Approval or Cancelled" — i.e. the Approved flag itself is protected from reversion, but nothing states that editing amount/vendor/GL after approval reverts that flag or forces resubmission. Since Xero's "approval" is a single status flag rather than a multi-approver chain to begin with, there's no "collected approvals" to invalidate in the way Ramp or Coupa mean it — this is a structurally different (simpler, weaker) model. **[INFERENCE layered on two sourced facts]**, not a single page stating "editing does not reset approval."
- **NetSuite**: same pattern — "After a bill is approved, the status can't later be changed to Pending Approval or Cancelled," and any role with Edit permission can edit the record regardless of approval status, with no separate locked-while-approved state documented. We checked the two most likely pages (native Vendor Bill Approval Workflow, and the SuiteFlow-based "NetSuite Approvals Workflow" SuiteApp) specifically for a reset-on-edit rule and found none in either. NetSuite explicitly positions **custom SuiteFlow workflows** as the place a business would build such a rule itself — it is not native. **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]**, with the strong caveat that NetSuite's own docs frame this as something you'd have to build, not something already built.

**Genuinely undocumented, no signal either way:**
- **Tipalti**: checked the two most on-point articles ("Manage approved bills," "Set up bill approvals and controls") directly and both are silent on this exact mechanic. Tipalti does document that post-approval edits are time-windowed (configurable to "during ERP accounting period," "+ month end close," or "at any time," and explicitly **not supported for PO-matched bills**) and are logged to an audit history — but never states whether the edit affects the approval status itself. [SOURCED for the time-window/audit facts](https://help.tipalti.com/hc/en-us/articles/40540365970583-Manage-approved-bills) / **[NOT DOCUMENTED]** for the reset question specifically.
- **Stampli**: no first-party page addresses this at all (checked 7 candidate pages). One blog post gives generic AP best-practice advice ("approval evidence should show what version the approver saw") that reads as buyer-education content, not a description of Stampli's own enforced behavior — explicitly not counted as a finding.
- **Melio**: the one adjacent fact found is about editing the *workflow rules* (not the bill) — "Bills currently pending approval retain their pending status; bills already approved or declined are not affected by edits to the workflow" — which doesn't answer the bill-field-edit question. Structurally, Melio prevents most of this scenario by construction (Approvers can't edit at all; Accountant/Contributor can only edit bills they created, which are unlikely to also be bills already approved by someone else) — but what happens if an **Admin** edits amount/vendor on a bill with partial approvals collected is undocumented.
- **QBO**: searched specifically (community-forum threads exist around adjacent complaints but are unresolved user reports, not documented product behavior, and were deliberately excluded as findings) — no help-center article addresses it.
- **Concur**: the closest documented mechanic is generic workflow step-progression logic ("the system may reset the status after the approval action occurs"), which describes transitions *between* steps, not a material-field-edit trigger. Notably, the exact concept you're asking about ("configure rules that determine whether the approval workflow restarts or continues when an approver edits invoice data") **does exist as a named, documented feature — but in SAP Ariba Invoicing, a different SAP product, not Concur.** This is a useful negative-adjacent finding: the pattern is established SAP product design language, just not confirmed as shipped in Concur Invoice specifically. **[NOT DOCUMENTED for Concur]**.
- **Bill.com**: **[NOT DOCUMENTED / COULD NOT VERIFY]** — help.bill.com unreachable; neither the payment-status-value docs nor the approve/deny API doc address it.

**Summary for the crux question:** across 10 products, exactly **one** (Ramp) has a clearly documented, unconditional "material edit hard-resets the approval chain" rule. **One more** (Coupa) has the mechanism built and named, but the automatic-trigger question is open. The rest are either structurally silent-edit-leaning (Xero, NetSuite — single status flag, no chain to invalidate) or simply undocumented (Tipalti, Stampli, Melio for the admin-edit case, QBO, Concur, Bill.com). This is a genuinely thin, inconsistent area of the market — treat "most AP tools don't clearly solve this" as a real finding, not a search failure.

## Strongest and weakest findings

**Strongest findings** (high confidence, directly sourced, clean answers):
1. **Ramp's approve-view field-locking and hard approval-reset rule** (§3, Bucket 1) — the single cleanest, most concrete, most decision-relevant finding in this whole research pass. Confirms your hypothesis precisely: amount/vendor/payment-details are locked from the approver's edit view and always editable-but-tightly-controlled elsewhere; editing those same fields is an unconditional, non-configurable trigger that restarts the approval chain from scratch. No ambiguity, two independent help articles corroborate each other.
2. **Melio's approver-scoping and self-approval-block** (§1, §5) — clean, explicit, help-center-sourced: approvers see only assigned bills, cannot edit at all, and self-approval is blocked with no override. The one weak spot is the sole-approver-removed edge case, which Melio's own docs contradict internally.
3. **QBO's custom-role assignment/creator-based scoping** (§1) — a genuinely useful, non-obvious finding: contrary to the plausible hypothesis that QBO permissions are purely feature/screen-based, its Advanced/Enterprise-Suite custom roles do implement real record-level scoping ("Approve" = assigned-only, "Create" = creator-only). This directly answers a question we were asked to specifically check.
4. **Coupa's named `restart_approvals` API action** (§3, Bucket 2) — strong evidence the "re-routing on change" pattern is real, designed, enterprise-market behavior, even though we couldn't confirm its automatic trigger condition.
5. **Tipalti's multi-entity/legal-entity user-access model** (§2) — the best-documented legal-entity scoping mechanism of any product researched, with a clear "default entity + entity switcher" pattern.

**Weakest findings** (real gaps, flagged rather than guessed around):
1. **Bill.com, across all five questions** — help.bill.com was unreachable for the entire session (TLS certificate failure); every Bill.com claim in this doc is either from public API/developer docs (schema, not UI behavior) or is explicitly marked undocumented. This is the single biggest hole in the research — if Bill.com's actual scoping behavior matters for your design, it needs a live account or an environment where help.bill.com resolves.
2. **The core "does editing invalidate collected approvals" question, for 6 of 10 products** (Tipalti, Stampli, Melio's admin-edit case, QBO, Concur, Bill.com) — genuinely undocumented, not a search failure; we checked the most on-point pages directly for each and confirmed their silence. This is itself the most important finding of the whole file: most AP products have not published a clear answer to the exact question you most need answered, which suggests either (a) it's genuinely inconsistent/ad hoc in their implementations, or (b) it's considered too fine-grained for public docs and only surfaces in support tickets or live product behavior.
3. **Stampli's help center is nearly empty publicly** (3 indexed articles total) — everything cited for Stampli is blog/marketing content, explicitly lower-confidence per your instructions. Treat all Stampli rows in this doc as directional, not authoritative.
4. **Coupa's actual admin/end-user UI documentation** (Compass) is entirely login-gated — everything sourced here is from public developer/API docs, which describe data model and available actions but not what a human approver's screen actually renders or defaults to.
5. **NetSuite's interaction between record-visibility Restrictions and approval-routing** — both mechanisms are well-documented individually, but no page states how they combine (e.g., does an Employee-restricted role only see bills routed to them, or bills restricted by the Employee field, or both/neither) — flagged as undocumented rather than assumed.
